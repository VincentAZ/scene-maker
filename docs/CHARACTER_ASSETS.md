# Realistic character library

The characters offered in the library are realistic, rigged, textured humans downloaded and converted at build time by `node scripts/build-content.mjs characters`. The converted files live in `content/characters/` (git-ignored, served at `/content/`); the committed catalogue is `shared/content/characters.json`, and every entry records its author, licence and source URL in `credit`. Only CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA and MIT content is shipped.

## Sources

- **Microsoft Rocketbox** (115 characters, [MIT](https://github.com/microsoft/Microsoft-Rocketbox/blob/master/LICENSE.md)): the complete avatar library, pinned to commit `0943055d`. Adults, children and professions (business, medical, chef, delivery, gardener, woodworker, construction, police, security, pilots, firefighters, soldiers, athletes). Display names, roles, collections and descriptions belong to Scene Maker; the ids come from the upstream folder names (`rocketbox-female-adult-01`). `Female_Party_01/02` exist under both `Adults` and `Professions` upstream, so a twin under another folder would carry its folder in its id; the Adults pair keep the plain ids. The two Professions copies hold only a stray facial FBX and are not converted.
- **Grab3D** (4 characters, [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/)): realistic rigged humans, each reviewed before it ships (see below).
- **Blender Studio** characters (CC BY 4.0) are being added as a further source module on the same pipeline.

Microsoft's MIT notice from the pinned commit is kept verbatim at [public/library/rocketbox/LICENSE.txt](../public/library/rocketbox/LICENSE.txt). A production or Unreal package of a scene that uses library models lists each one with its author, licence and source in `assets/library/CREDITS.txt`, and carries the Rocketbox licence beside it when a Rocketbox character is among them, since the models travel baked into `scene.glb`.

Sources that cannot be bundled stay with the creator's own account: Adobe Mixamo characters and animations are imported by the creator through the guided round trip (see below) and are never shipped with the app.

## Pipeline

`scripts/content/characters.mjs` is a dispatcher: every module in `scripts/content/characters/` exports `build({ out, cache, root, log })` and returns catalogue entries; the modules run in name order and their entries are concatenated. `CHARACTER_SOURCES=rocketbox,grab3d` limits a run to those sources and keeps the other sources' entries from the current catalogue; for a module that exports `idPrefix` (Rocketbox: `rocketbox-`), the entries it no longer builds are dropped too, so a removed or renamed character does not linger. Shared helpers (download pool with retries, the Blender runner, GLB JSON rewriting, the Biped clavicle fix, the preview crop) live in `scripts/content/lib.mjs`.

The Rocketbox module (`scripts/content/characters/rocketbox.mjs`) downloads each avatar's non-facial FBX, its preview render and its colour, normal and opacity textures (including variant sheets such as the soldiers' `_color_acu`; specular and wrinkle maps are skipped) into `content/.cache/rocketbox/<Avatar>/`, then:

1. shrinks and re-encodes the 4K TGA textures in Blender (JPEG ≤2k for colour, ≤1k for normals; PNG ≤1k for the RGBA hair/lash sheet) and renames the references inside the binary FBX in place;
2. converts the FBX with the app's own importer, `engine/convert.py` (bind frame restored from the FBX bind pose, no scale or axis change: the centimetre units survive as a 0.01 scale on the Biped root);
3. corrects the GLB: every material becomes dielectric (Rocketbox ships them fully metallic, which renders as dark chrome), hair and lashes are alpha-tested so their cards never sort over the face, the empty "Take 001" is dropped, the children's `Bip02` prefix becomes `Bip01`, and the clavicles are re-parented from the neck to the chest while keeping their world binds, so a neck bend no longer swings the shoulders;
4. crops the upstream preview render to the figure as the 384 px card;
5. records the character's standing height, measured from the converted file's bind pose (hats and helmets included), as `height` in the catalogue. The studio shows a character at its recorded height, so the children stand about 1.4 m tall and the adults 1.72–1.87 m; a character without one (an import, a Mixamo download) is sized to 1.85 m as before.

Conversions are idempotent (a GLB is rebuilt only when it or its `converted-v<version>` stamp is missing), run six side by side, and `CHARACTERS_ONLY=<id>,<id>` limits a rebuild. The library was built with the bundled Blender 4.5 LTS; set `BLENDER_PATH` to pin it. Every character is recognised by the studio's rig mapping (3ds Max Biped names) with all fifteen body roles, stands on the floor and takes the studio actions; `tests/characters.test.ts` checks the catalogue and, when the files are built, every GLB.

## Mixamo

Adobe forbids redistributing Mixamo files, so the studio never bundles them. **Generate with AI · Rig with Mixamo** prepares any character, including the realistic library, as an unrigged FBX, opens mixamo.com and watches the Downloads folder. The downloads can be imported as one character (the file downloaded With Skin plus its animations) or, with **Each file becomes its own character**, as a batch of up to 40 files: every skinned file becomes a character named after its file ("Ch14_nonPBR" → "Ch14") and the skinless animation files after it become its actions. A file that is neither (a model without a skeleton, or one with nothing in it) is refused by name, and a batch that fails part-way keeps none of its files. In both modes the one-frame T-pose take of a character download is left out of the actions. Files chosen through the file dialog are sent in the order they were downloaded (their modified time), because a dialog lists them alphabetically and an animation file does not name its character; ticked downloads keep the order they were ticked in. A batch places its first character in the scene and adds the others to My assets, in one undo step. Card thumbnails are rendered in the browser.

## Retired stylized library

Earlier versions bundled 32 stylized low-poly humanoids from [Quaternius's Ultimate Animated Character Pack](https://quaternius.com/packs/ultimatedanimatedcharacter.html) (CC0 1.0; licence preserved at [public/library/quaternius/LICENSE.txt](../public/library/quaternius/LICENSE.txt), per-file provenance in [character-sources.json](character-sources.json)). They are no longer offered in the library but still back the [motion bank](MOTION_ASSETS.md) and the character maker's starting looks: `scripts/prepare-character-library.ts` restores their bind pose from the skin inverse matrices and maps Body/Abdomen to the studio's hips and spine, and `scripts/render-character-library.py` renders their cards. Production and Unreal packages keep the used Quaternius GLBs under `assets/quaternius/` with the licence.

## Grab3D realistic characters

Four rigged humans from [Grab3D](https://grab3d.com/models/characters/) join the Rocketbox cast: Man in a T-shirt, Woman in a sports top, Pirate captain and Ninja. Every package is dedicated to the public domain (CC0 1.0) by Grab3D, Alexander Popov's site; the models are AI-assisted (mesh and textures from Meshy's image-to-3D tool, cleaned up, scaled, textured and checked in Blender, with the skeleton and skinning from Meshy Auto-Rig, as each package's `LICENSE.txt` discloses), so the catalogue credits them as "Alexander Popov (Grab3D), AI-assisted" with each model page as the source, and each description ends "An AI-assisted model." (the library search finds them by it; the cards show only the name and collection).

`scripts/content/characters/grab3d.mjs` downloads the rigged GLB with its `manifest.json` and `LICENSE.txt` into `content/.cache/grab3d/<slug>/` and refuses any package whose licence text is not the CC0 dedication. Grab3D's download links carry no version and the author does revise packages (the Samurai and the Viking gained finger bones after release), so the cast also records the SHA-256 of the reviewed GLB and `LICENSE.txt`; a file that differs is refused ("Grab3D changed …; review it before it ships") until it has been looked at again. The converter uses glTF Transform, with the CLI, its libraries and the meshopt simplifier each pinned to an exact version so a rebuild reproduces the shipped files: welded and simplified to at most 120k triangles (meshopt keeps the skin weights), the bundled walk and run clips dropped, textures kept at 2048 px, the spine renamed the Mixamo way (`Spine`, `Spine1`, `Spine2` counting up from the hips), the rest pose re-posed onto straight vertical legs, a knuckle joint added to each hand (below) and the material made dielectric unless the outfit carries metal. A character that cannot be built is reported and left out, and the others still build. Thumbnails are EEVEE renders of the converted file, framed like the Rocketbox previews; the script asks for EEVEE by the name the running Blender uses (`BLENDER_EEVEE_NEXT` in 4.2–4.5, `BLENDER_EEVEE` from 5.0).

`CHARACTER_SOURCES=grab3d node scripts/build-content.mjs characters` rebuilds this source alone and keeps every other source's entries; `CHARACTERS_ONLY=grab3d-pirate` limits it to one character.

The skeleton is Meshy's 24-joint humanoid: hips, a three-segment spine, neck, head, shoulders, arms, hands, legs, feet and toes. There are no face or finger bones (of the candidates only the Samurai and the Viking carry finger bones, added in Blender by the author). The studio transfers a recording against an anatomical rest of each limb, and it straightens the wrist of that rest from the joints under the hand. Meshy's hands hang bent 15–25° at the wrist in the rest pose, and with nothing under the hand that bend stayed in the reference, so every recording bent the wrist that much further: in Wave hello the raised hand folded sideways, 25° away from where a Rocketbox hand points at the same moment. The converter adds one unweighted joint under each hand at the centre of the knuckles, measured from the skin as the median of the hand's vertices across the first finger segments (a median, so the thumb lying beside them does not pull it; on the Rocketbox and Samurai hands, whose finger joints are known, this lies within 6° of the real knuckles). The studio then straightens the wrist on it as it does on finger joints, and through the wave the raised hand now points within 12° of where the Rocketbox hand points. A single joint names no thumb, so the forearm keeps the roll of the rest pose, in which these four hold their palms toward the thighs. The fingers keep the relaxed curl of the rest pose in every action, since there is nothing to move them.

The ankle joints sit a little up the shin, which is why the tests check the floor contact of the mesh rather than the joint height. Meshy's rest pose also stands with the knees bent (9–34°) and the legs apart (5–10° off vertical); the studio grounds a character by the extent of its rest pose and then plays recordings whose legs are straight, so the feet sank below the floor by whatever the bend and the splay had taken from the height (the Pirate captain reached 6 cm and was flagged as overlapping the backdrop the moment he was placed). The converter therefore straightens each leg and swings it vertical from the hip, keeps the foot's orientation, skins the vertices into the new rest pose and rebuilds the leg joints' inverse bind matrices; the tests check the straight legs in the file and the idle floor contact.

Every candidate was converted and reviewed as a thumbnail, as face and hand close-ups rendered in Blender, and in the studio: a walk route to the upholstered armchair, Wave hello and Sit down, with stills of the whole body, the upper body and the waving hand taken from the character's front three-quarter, and the wave compared with a Rocketbox character at the same moments. The creator wants nothing that looks fake, so only convincing characters ship:
